## Decision

Each leaf states one complete decision. Read alone, its Decision says what is chosen, fully enough that the owner could ratify or reject it without opening any child. A child records a different question that its parent leaves open, one that could be flipped while the parent stands. A leaf that finishes its parent (fills in what the parent's Decision gestures at, supplies its numbers or its list of cases, restates or details it, names one aspect of it) is not a child: it folds back into the parent, or becomes spec.

Two tests settle it. Hide the child: if the parent no longer says something whole, the child was finishing it, so fold. Flip the child: if the parent goes false or empty, the child was an aspect, so fold.

## Why

The tree's shape is what makes blast radius and precedence computable (A9). An unfinished parent can't be ratified on its own, and a child that finishes it adds a leaf to every radius and a place for the parent's rule to be contradicted, without adding a decision anyone could take separately.

## Consequences

- `dte fold <child>` moves the child's Decision and Consequences into the parent, its citations and children with them, and writes a `folded` ledger line.
- A parent that cites its children to complete itself fails validate already, since a leaf cites only up its lineage (B49).
- One leaf may govern several things when turning it off for one would break the others; those stay one leaf. A leaf holding two calls that could be flipped separately splits in two.
- Things that were ever reversed independently stay separate.
