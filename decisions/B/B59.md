## Decision

Every leaf records when it was made (`date`) and whether a human settled it (`ratified_by`). `made_by` is written only when a human, or a human with an agent, made the leaf; absent, it reads as `ai`. Who wrote it is git's record: the tool writes no `by`, and reads one an import carries. AI and joint leaves may be in effect without ratification, but validation surfaces every unratified one on every run until a human fills the field.

## Why

A3 requires provenance captured at the moment of decision. Git already records who committed each leaf and with which agent, at that moment, so a `by` field is a second copy that can disagree; and since agents write most leaves, the common case should cost nothing to write and the human case should stand out. Requiring ratification before a leaf takes effect would block agents mid-task and push them toward not recording decisions at all, which is worse.

## Consequences

- `dte validate` names the unratified leaves of rings A and B and counts the rest; `dte unratified` lists them all.
- Ratification is a one-field edit by a human, or `dte ratify`.

## Contest

Carried from B7, which this leaf supersedes.

### Contest 2026-10-31 by Claude Opus 5.5: variant wins

Rubric: A3 provenance.
- keep: made_by on every leaf. Cost: 62 of DTE's 71 leaves and nearly all of Solenoid's 195 carried `made_by: ai`, a line that says nothing the absence couldn't.
- opposite: no provenance fields, git only. Cost: A3 asks who, and git names a committer, not the model or the human who made the call.
- deletion: no B rule; A3 alone. Cost: nothing says what a leaf must carry.
- variant: by, date and ratified_by always; made_by only when not ai. Cost: an old tree runs dte upgrade once. Wins: same information, one line less per leaf, and the human-made leaves are the ones that say so.
Verdict: variant.
