## Decision

Bodies are edited by hand, never by a command. Every such edit adds a History line, or, when one wording-only sweep touches many leaves, one line in the editorial log (`decisions/EDITORIAL`) records the sweep and the body it left on each leaf. `validate` compares each changed leaf's body to HEAD, marks it `body changed` in the changed-leaves block, and warns unless a History line HEAD lacks is present or the editorial log vouches for that exact body.

## Why

`set` logs title changes, but a reworded Decision leaves no trace, so a ratifier cannot see that a leaf changed after drafting. A3 wants who changed what to be visible, and detection catches stray edits by human or agent. A vocabulary sweep across a hundred leaves is one event, though, and the same History line on each one turns History into a dev log about wording instead of a record of the decision.

## Consequences

- No `reword` command; `dte editorial --by <who> --note "..."` writes the sweep's line.
- A later edit to a swept leaf changes its body hash, so it warns again.
- A History reset (a ratification, a rewrite) counts as logged, since its new line is one HEAD lacks.

### Contest 2026-10-31 by Claude Opus 5.5: variant wins

Rubric: A3 provenance.
- keep: a History line per edited leaf. Cost: Solenoid's tree-wide rename touched 129 leaves and wrote the same line 129 times; clearing a History read as an unlogged edit because the check counted lines.
- opposite: no logging of body edits. Cost: a reworded Decision reaches ratification unseen, against A3.
- deletion: git alone. Cost: the ratifier reads the leaf, not the log.
- variant: a History line, or one editorial-log line per sweep pinned to each body's hash; the check compares against HEAD's lines. Cost: one small file. Wins: same visibility, History stays about the decision.
Verdict: variant.
